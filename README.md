## Hi there 👋 I'm Yu-Shu

## What drives me

I cannot quite explain where my curiosity about life comes from. I simply want to understand how living systems work, and that curiosity keeps me asking questions and learning. I also hope that what we discover can improve people’s quality of life.

My research has taken me from designing and synthesizing candidate anticancer compounds to using dCas9-based tools to investigate gene regulation. In my doctoral research, I study insect telomerase, including its function, purification, and RNA biogenesis. In humans, telomerase plays important roles in telomere maintenance, stem cell biology, and cancer, connecting many of the questions that interest me.

Working across chemistry and biology has taught me to approach problems from different perspectives. I am now building my computational skills, from fundamental algorithms to the analysis of large biological datasets. Together, these experiences have given me a foundation for learning unfamiliar methods, exploring questions with no clear answer, and solving biological problems from different perspectives.

I enjoy finding connections between fields and using them to ask new questions. I hope to bring these approaches together to investigate health and disease and explore paths toward better treatments, so more people can spend meaningful time with those they love.
